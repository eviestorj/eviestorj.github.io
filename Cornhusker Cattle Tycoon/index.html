📰 Why this game matters

Family cattle ranches are disappearing. America has lost roughly four in 10 cattle ranches since 1978, and Nebraska alone dropped from 17,707 beef cow operations in 2017 to 14,834 in 2022, according to USDA census numbers reported by the Flatwater Free Press. Drought has sped up that decline. In late summer 2026, the Nebraska Farm Bureau reported that 86% of the state's cattle were in drought areas, most pastures were rated poor or very poor, and hay supplies were down, pushing ranchers to cull cows instead of growing their herds. At the same time, UNL Extension educator Aaron Berger warns that costs like fuel, equipment and labor have climbed faster than the prices ranchers get for their cattle, and just four meatpacking companies now handle most U.S. beef processing, which leaves ranchers with fewer buyers. Cornhusker Cattle Tycoon puts you in the boots of a young Nebraska rancher facing those same pressures, from drought and rising costs to tight markets and hard choices about whether to sell or keep cattle, so you can see why so many family ranches don't survive and what it takes to keep one going.

Sources: Flatwater Free Press · UNL BeefWatch · Nebraska Farm Bureau
